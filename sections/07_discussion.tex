\section{Discussion}

\subsection{From Pairwise Evidence to Scene-Scale Geometry}

UAV-TAlign-12K reveals a transition in the UAV infrared-visible alignment problem. Infrared-specific
and modern matchers generate homographies for nearly every pair, providing abundant geometric
evidence across the official split. The evidence nevertheless varies in spatial support,
cross-modal agreement, and consistency with other frames from the same scene. Scene consensus is
therefore a distinct alignment stage: it converts heterogeneous frame estimates into one scene
transform that can be used as a common geometric product.

The fixed eight-scene UAV-TAlign-1K-Lite ablation identifies robust consensus as the dominant
module-level contribution. Relative to pairwise evidence alone, consensus improves edge and
gradient agreement while sharply reducing severe-outlier incidence and transform dispersion.
Cross-modal verification then selects the canonical high-confidence products. The combined design links local
correspondence evidence with the scene-scale output required by UAV inspection and multimodal
sensing.

\subsection{Coverage-Aware Operating Analysis}

The reliability--coverage profile adds an operating dimension to fixed-threshold evaluation. The
canonical point provides nine high-confidence products covering 74.4\% of evaluated pairs, while
the score-ranked sweep exposes broader coverage regimes. This representation allows an application
to select scene-product coverage according to its quality requirements without changing the
pairwise matcher or consensus estimator.

The controlled analyses probe the underlying diagnostics. With one fold withheld, recomputed
consensus yields a 1.39-pixel median of scene-wise mean displacements, characterizing sensitivity to the
selected evidence. Translation, rotation, and scale perturbations progressively reduce the mean
joint edge/gradient signal, characterizing its response to geometric disturbance. These observations
support the use of consensus stability and image agreement as complementary diagnostics.

\subsection{Benchmark Extensibility}

The benchmark separates pairwise correspondence, scene aggregation, and cross-modal verification
through a manifest-based interface. Future matchers and consensus estimators can therefore be
studied on the same captured-data split. The scene homography describes a dominant planar or
far-field alignment; spatially varying geometry calls for local or nonrigid representations.
The reported operating profiles characterize relative image agreement and evidence consistency
within the 15 captured scenes.

Deterministic evidence scheduling provides a reproducible default for this shared interface. The
multi-seed study shows that random selection changes the product status of a small set of
near-threshold scenes, whereas accepted-frame support remains comparatively stable. This result
positions scheduling as a reproducibility mechanism and leaves robust consensus and cross-modal
verification as the principal scene-product components.
